# Security model

IntentMesh constrains how semantic proposals can become transaction authority. Its security argument depends on explicit admission, deterministic decisions, separate human approval, restricted signing, and observed postconditions. The controls below describe the implementation and its bounded demonstrations. They are not an independent audit, a financial-safety guarantee, or production custody validation.

The historical [security profile](../evidence/sepolia/security-profile.v1.json) defines a synthetic Sepolia experiment. The [finality evidence](../evidence/sepolia/finality-evidence.v1.json) records chain observations. Neither artifact alone proves that every off-chain control executed correctly.

**Threat: model output acquires financial authority.** A plausible interpretation or injected instruction could attempt to change the amount, asset, beneficiary, permitted venue, or policy limits. The control is a deterministic admission boundary with controlled field authority and provenance. Model proposals require semantic confirmation and cannot supply protected values merely by presenting valid JSON. Missing or inconsistent required authority fails admission.

Evidence includes the implemented authority checks and historical local mutation and prompt-injection tests. The public trace begins after this semantic stage and does not independently demonstrate resistance to arbitrary natural-language attacks. Residual risk includes incomplete invariants, parser behavior, and shared assumptions in implementation and tests. Deterministic code can still contain bugs.

**Threat: specialist or tool data is mistaken for permission.** A service can return attractive but ineligible evidence, stale values, or altered artifacts. MCP capabilities are bounded and read-only in this PoC. Profiles, authenticated communication, source pins, freshness rules, and evidence validation constrain inputs; deterministic Risk and Strategy outputs remain non-authorizing proposals or evidence.

Local malformed, stale, hostile, and provenance cases provide bounded acceptance evidence. The Gamma correction demonstrates the distinction between valid evidence and an eligible plan. Residual risk remains in trusted fixture construction, clocks, service configuration, and external evidence quality. Synthetic signed data does not establish real-market accuracy, and authentication does not make a source's claim true.

**Threat: ambiguous values or artifact substitution changes the operation.** Exact integer units, network-qualified identities, schema checks, canonical artifact identities, and downstream hash comparisons bind a plan to its inputs. The recorded deposit uses 10,000,000,000 smallest imUSDC units. Source SHA-256 digests and canonical JSON/Keccak identities serve distinct purposes.

The copied evidence exposes source digests, artifact bindings, and a linked trace for inspection. Residual limits are significant: hash consistency is not factual truth, authorship, or approval. The schema-version approval binding hashes schema identifiers, not entire schema files. Canonicalization also does not recover duplicate keys already collapsed by an ordinary JSON parser; whole-ingress duplicate-key rejection is not established here.

**Threat: an attractive proposal bypasses policy or changes selection.** Finite construction limits the available operation shapes. Deterministic policy filters candidates before a risk-first selector compares eligible plans. Gamma cannot become selectable through a high synthetic yield claim. Stable ordering and tie-breaking keep identical admitted inputs from depending on proposal order.

Historical planning and mutation tests exercise policy rejection, eligible selection, and altered evidence or calldata. The public trace records Alpha selection. Residual risk includes incorrect rules, incomplete candidate coverage, and misleading synthetic metrics. These controls define a constrained search space; they cannot establish that the chosen strategy is optimal or appropriate outside that space.

**Threat: approval applies to different execution conditions.** Simulation precedes execution approval. The approval profile binds intent, full selected plan, simulation, policy bundle, schema-version identifiers, chain, signer, beneficiary, nonce, and validity interval. Confirmation of semantic meaning is a separate earlier action and cannot authorize execution.

Local simulation executes and restores an Anvil snapshot. Public preparation uses read-only call simulation and gas estimation at a recorded block with an exact allowance override. Historical tests exercise binding mutations; the public trace records simulation and approval stages. Residual risk includes state changes after simulation and the narrower public simulation observations. Simulation is conditional evidence, not a prediction that all later state will remain identical.

**Threat: a valid signature is reused or accepted after expiry.** The approval gateway and signer check validity windows and replay state. A file-backed registry transitions through available, reserved, and consumed states; the public workflow additionally constrains the exact allowance and deposit nonces. Historical replay and restart tests support single-process persistence behavior.

Atomic file replacement does not provide distributed locking or concurrency coordination. Crashes and uncertain broadcasts require reconciliation before reuse. There is no production nonce service or complete automated recovery system. A consumed approval or an included transaction cannot be made unused by deleting an error message or regenerating an artifact.

**Threat: signing or submission escapes the approved operation.** A separate restricted signer process unlocks an encrypted software keystore interactively. Models receive no private key, and A2A/MCP services have no signing access. The signer revalidates the operation; the submission boundary parses signed EIP-1559 bytes, recovers the signer, and compares transaction fields against the expected request before broadcasting.

The historical workflow records distinct allowance and deposit signature-validation stages followed by public transaction hashes. These are accompanied by local mutation checks in the underlying implementation, not a public independent reimplementation. Residual risk includes the host, keystore handling, software dependencies, and shared validation code. Process separation is not HSM/MPC custody. Submission infrastructure also needs RPC access; excluding keys from model input does not imply that every process is credential-free.

**Threat: the experiment reaches another network or transfers unintended value.** The profile pins Ethereum Sepolia, chain ID 11155111 and its genesis hash, uses a dedicated testnet deployer, permits only the synthetic contracts and bounded actions, limits gas and fees, disallows native-value transfers, and prohibits unlimited approval. The historical record contains eight setup transactions and the two exact workflow transactions.

Mainnet exclusion is enforced by workflow/configuration gates and account-use discipline. It is not an intrinsic property of the EOA or the deployed bytecode. The token itself can represent an unlimited allowance even though this workflow forbids one. A person using the key or contracts outside these controls is outside the demonstrated boundary.

**Threat: inclusion is reported as economic success.** Verification binds receipts to expected operations, waits for finalized observations, and checks code, logs, balances, allowances, and configuration. The public record reports ten successful transactions, four runtime code checks, and thirteen state checks at one common finalized block using two RPC sources.

A successful receipt is not a successful economic result. The bounded postconditions supply the additional observation, with source agreement recorded explicitly. Two endpoints do not prove complete infrastructure independence. Hash-linked off-chain records are neither externally anchored attestations nor proof of withheld approval preimages. A failed postcondition cannot reverse an included transaction. Synthetic contracts enforce their own small rules; they do not intrinsically enforce the full IntentMesh approval or policy model. Further scope limits are recorded in [Limitations](LIMITATIONS.md).
